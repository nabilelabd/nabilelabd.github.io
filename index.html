What it is

Three buttons sized to your own bottles. Tap one, the vessel fills, and everyone you've added sees it happen.

Hydration apps are easy to abandon because nothing is at stake. Sipp adds the one thing that keeps a habit alive — other people watching. Friends connect by privately swapping a code, and the leaderboard ranks by how close each person got to their own goal, so someone chasing 4,000 ml never beats someone chasing 2,000 just by being thirstier.

Engineering

The parts that were actually hard

Sync

A tap can't wait for the network

Logging water had to work on the metro with no signal, so every entry is written locally first with a client-generated UUID and pushed later. The naive version of that loses data: a tap that lands while a sync is in flight isn't in the response coming back, so overwriting local state with the server's view silently discards it.

The fix was a push-then-pull-then-merge cycle where anything still unsynced is carried forward rather than replaced. I proved the old behaviour was broken with a standalone model of the sync semantics before changing it.

Security

Friends see exactly what they're allowed to

Access rules live in Postgres as row-level security policies, not in the app. A stranger can't read your profile, your drinks, or even confirm your account exists — and that holds no matter what the client asks for, because the database refuses rather than the app declining to display it.

Invite-code lookup runs through a security-definer function so a code can find one person without exposing the profiles table to the world.

Product

The day starts at 4am

A drink at 1am belongs to the night that's still going, not to a new day nobody has woken up for. Every date calculation in the app runs on that boundary — totals, streaks, history, the leaderboard.

It sounds like a detail until you handle it wrong and someone's streak breaks while they're still awake.

Motion

The water is drawn, not played

The fill isn't a video or a Lottie file. Two sine waves are recomputed every frame on the UI thread and clipped to whichever vessel you picked, so the surface moves independently of what React is doing and never stutters mid-scroll.

Passing the goal breaks water over both rims in a sheet that runs down the outside and pools underneath — then settles to a trickle after a few seconds, because a permanent full-rate cascade is a battery bill that stops meaning anything.

Shipping

Getting it past Apple

Plenty of people can get an app onto a simulator. The distance between that and a listing in the App Store is where most of them stop — and it's most of what this project actually taught.

Sign in with Apple

Required once you offer any third-party login. Wired to the same Supabase identity, with the display name captured on first authorisation — Apple never sends it twice.

Account deletion, in app

A hard requirement. Runs through a serverless function so the account and every row it owns go together, immediately, not by email request.

Review, and a rejection

Came back under Guideline 2.1 for missing reviewer access. Resolved with a pre-populated demo account, screen recordings, and a full written disclosure of every external service.

Everything else

Privacy policy, export compliance, age rating, medical-device declaration, EU trader status, regional availability, TestFlight, and per-device screenshot specs.
